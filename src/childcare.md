If you take away one thing from this page, make it this: **get on the waitlists early.** Childcare is the piece of a PCS that most often runs behind the move itself. Here's how the options at and around Scott fit together, in the order most families tackle them.

- **Child Development Centers.** The 375th FSS says Scott's CDC program is accredited by the National Association for the Education of Young Children (NAEYC) and certified by DoD. The Home Life page says both centers offer weekly care, plus hourly care when space allows.
- **Family Child Care (FCC).** Care happens in certified providers' homes on or off base. The Home Life page says FCC "has the ability to tailor care to fit every schedule, deployment, or special need." Military OneSource says FCC regulations cap a home at six children under age 8, with no more than two under age 2.
- **School Age Program.** Before- and after-school care plus a summer camp for ages 5 to 12. Youth members ages 9 to 17 can also use the Youth Center's open recreation program.

## How the MilitaryChildCare.com waitlist works

Full-time on-base care is requested through [MilitaryChildCare.com](https://militarychildcare.com/) (MCC), the DoD request-for-care system. From the [MCC FAQ](https://public.militarychildcare.csd.disa.mil/mcc-central/mcchome/faq):

- **Your place depends on two things:** your DoD priority (based on sponsor type and, if married, spouse employment) and the date you submitted the request.
- **You get an estimate, not a number.** MCC shows an Anticipated Placement Time (APT) for each request.
- **Cast a wide net.** You can request several programs at once. For FCC, choosing "Any Provider" puts you on the list for every FCC provider in your search area.
- **Watch your email.** When an offer comes, you have **2 business days** to respond, and reconfirmation notices give you 15 days.
- **Hourly or drop-in care** isn't managed through MCC. Contact the program directly.

## If on-base care isn't available yet

- **MCCYN fee assistance.** Military Child Care in Your Neighborhood helps eligible families who can't access installation care pay for an approved community provider. [Child Care Aware of America](https://www.childcareaware.org/fee-assistancerespite/military-families/air-force/) says Air and Space Force families within 15 miles or 20 minutes of an installation program must have an active MCC request. Providers need a state license, an inspection within the last 12 months, and national accreditation. Child Care Aware also says that starting in June 2026, administration of the Department of the Air Force program is moving to a MilitaryChildCare.com team, with families notified in phases.
- **Help with a caregiver's travel.** If full-day care at a military-run center at your new duty station isn't available within 30 days of your date care needed, a DoD pilot program can reimburse up to **$500** (domestic PCS) for commercial travel for a designated caregiver. It runs through September 30, 2027 and has eligibility rules ([MCC travel reimbursement](https://public.militarychildcare.csd.disa.mil/mcc-central/mcchome/travel-reimbursement)).
- **Hourly and babysitting care.** Military OneSource gives eligible families free access to an online database of more than a million caregivers. The subscription is free, but you pay the caregiver directly, and DoD doesn't cover background-check costs ([Military OneSource](https://www.militaryonesource.mil/benefits/military-child-care-services/)).

## Checking an off-base provider in Illinois

Before you tour a daycare or in-home provider off base, look them up in the [Illinois DCFS Day Care Provider Lookup](https://sunshine.dcfs.illinois.gov/content/licensing/daycare/providerlookup.aspx). You can search by name, city, county or ZIP, and only currently licensed providers appear. The DCFS [compliance search](https://sunshine.dcfs.illinois.gov/Content/Licensing/Daycare/MonitoringReports.aspx) shows licensing compliance information gathered since January 1, 2010. Questions go to the DCFS day care information line at 1-877-746-0829.

## Money help during the move

- **Child Care for PCS (Air and Space Force families):** the [Air & Space Forces Aid Society](https://afas.org/ccfpcs/) pays **$200 per dependent child up to age 12, up to $1,000 per household**, by Zelle or ACH, and the money can go toward any childcare option. Active-duty members apply within 60 days of arrival or departure, with a copy of their military ID and PCS orders that list the children. *Checked October 18, 2026.* The M&FRC (618-256-8668) can help.

## More support for parents

- **New Parent Support Program:** free, voluntary help for expectant parents and parents of children up to age 3, including home visits, classes and playgroups. Call 618-256-7203 (Scott AFB Home Life).
- **Exceptional Family Member Program - Family Support:** help for families with special needs, including respite care and referrals. Call 618-256-4746.
- **Military OneSource:** 800-342-9647, 24/7.
